Most new freelancers set a rate by guessing, or by dividing a salary by 2,080 hours. That ignores the tax you pay yourself, the weeks you don't bill, and costs an employer would have covered. Enter the take-home income you want and this calculator works backward to the hourly and day rate that gets you there. It runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Hours you actually invoice. Sales, admin and bookkeeping time usually fall outside this number, so 20 to 30 is a common assumption for a full-time week.
The default self-employment tax set-aside is 15.3% × 92.35% ≈ 14.1% of net profit (below the Social Security limit). Your income tax rate depends on your income, filing status, deductions and state, so the 12% default is only a placeholder. Replace it with your own figure.

How it works

  1. Weeks worked = 52 minus your weeks off. Billable hours = billable hours per week × weeks worked.
  2. Pre-tax profit needed = take-home income ÷ (1 − combined tax set-aside %). If you want $80,000 in your pocket and set aside 26.1%, you need about $108,254 of profit before tax.
  3. Revenue needed = pre-tax profit + business expenses + retirement contributions + health costs.
  4. Hourly rate = revenue needed ÷ billable hours. Day rate = revenue needed ÷ (billable hours ÷ billable hours per day).

Assumptions to understand

  • Taxes are a set-aside, not a calculation. The self-employment tax part follows the IRS rates: 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) applied to 92.35% of net profit, per the IRS self-employment tax page. The Social Security part stops at $184,500 of combined wages and self-employment earnings in 2026 (IRS Topic 751). The calculator applies a flat percentage, so it slightly overstates the rate at high incomes.
  • Retirement and health costs are treated as extra costs on top of your take-home goal, and the tax set-aside is applied only to the take-home part. Many of these costs may be deductible, which can lower your real tax bill, so this leans conservative. A tax professional can tell you what applies to you.
  • Take-home means money you can spend. If you'd rather count retirement savings as part of your income, set that line to $0.
  • Late or unpaid invoices and scope creep are not modeled. Many freelancers add a cushion for them; that's a judgment call, not an IRS number.

FAQ

Why is my hourly rate so much higher than a salary divided by 2,080?

2,080 is 40 hours × 52 weeks, which assumes you bill every hour and take no unpaid time off. Freelancers don't bill every hour, don't get paid vacation, and pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare tax, so the same income needs a higher rate.

How many billable hours should I assume?

It depends on your work. Unbilled time for finding clients, proposals, invoicing and admin is real. There's no official number; start with your own time tracking and adjust this input.

What tax percentage should I use?

For self-employment tax, about 14.1% of net profit is the standard IRS-based figure while you're under the Social Security limit. For income tax, use your own estimate from last year's return or a tax professional. Our self-employment tax calculator gives the exact SE tax.

Should I charge hourly, daily or per project?

That's a business choice. The hourly and day rates here give you a floor, so any project price can be checked against how many hours it will take.

Does this include state taxes?

Only if you include them in the income tax set-aside %. The calculator doesn't know your state.
