Keeper vs TurboTax Self-Employed (Now Premium) for Freelancers

If you search for “TurboTax Self-Employed” today, you land on a different product name. Intuit’s discontinued products page says TurboTax Self-Employed was discontinued in 2023 and replaced by TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium, which now covers self-employment, investments and rentals in one tier. So the real comparison for a freelancer is Keeper versus TurboTax Premium: a year-round deduction tracker that files your return with a tax pro’s signature, versus tax software you fill in yourself and pay for when you file.

The short answer

  • Pick Keeper if your income is mostly 1099 work with business expenses spread across your cards, you want deductions sorted all year instead of in April, and you want a pro to review and sign the return. Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year with a federal return and up to 2 state returns included.
  • Pick TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium if you’re comfortable doing your own return, you also have stock, crypto or rental income, and you’d rather pay once at filing time than subscribe. It lists at $139 federal plus $64 per state.
  • Check IRS Free File first if your income is low. Last season the IRS’s Free File guided software was for an adjusted gross income of $95,000 or less; the program is closed now and the IRS says to check back in January 2027.

Who should pick which

  • Writers, designers and developers with mostly 1099 income and lots of small expenses: Keeper. Year-round tracking catches the software, phone and home-office costs you’d otherwise reconstruct in April (see the freelance tax deductions checklist).
  • Freelancers with brokerage, crypto or rental income too: TurboTax DIY Premium, unless you want a pro to sign the return, in which case compare Keeper Premium ($399) with TurboTax Expert Assist Premium ($278 with one state).
  • Two-state filers: Keeper Standard; it includes up to 2 state returns in the $199.
  • Floridians and others with no state return to file: TurboTax DIY Premium at $139 is cheaper if you’re confident doing it yourself.
  • Your first year freelancing: either works; the bigger risk is not paying estimated taxes. Start with our tax set-aside calculator.

Tax points that apply either way

  • Report all your income, form or no form. The IRS Form 1099-K page says payment apps and marketplaces must send a 1099-K when payments for goods or services top $20,000 in more than 200 transactions, but they may send one for less. IRS Publication 334 says the 1099-NEC reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025. Either way, the income is taxable (see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K).
  • Self-employment tax is the big one. Publication 334 says you owe it if net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more, at 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare). Our self-employment tax calculator runs the numbers.
  • Pay as you go. The IRS estimated taxes page says sole proprietors generally must make estimated payments if they expect to owe $1,000 or more when they file. See the 2027 due dates.
  • Part of the cost is deductible. Publication 334 says you can deduct on Schedule C the cost of preparing the part of your return that relates to your business.

Our take

For a typical solo freelancer with 1099 income and one state return, Keeper (affiliate link) Standard at $199 a year costs about the same as TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium ($203) and gives you more: expenses tracked all year and a return reviewed and signed by a tax pro. Its 14-day trial lets you connect your accounts and see what deductions it finds before you pay. TurboTax Premium is the better buy if you have investments or a rental (Keeper charges $399 for those), if you don’t file a state return, or if you simply prefer to do your own return and pay once a year. FAQ

Is TurboTax Self-Employed still available?

Not under that name. Intuit's discontinued-products page says TurboTax Self-Employed was discontinued in 2023 and replaced by TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium (and Live Assisted Self-Employed by Expert Assist Premium). Premium covers Schedule C self-employment income along with investments and rentals.

Which is cheaper for a freelancer with one state return?

They're about the same. As of Nov 9, 2026, Keeper Standard is $199 a year and includes a federal return and up to 2 state returns. TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium lists at $139 federal plus $64 per state, so $203 with one state. TurboTax's pages still show its tax-year-2025 prices and say the final price is set when you file. With two states Keeper is cheaper ($199 vs $267).

Does Keeper do my taxes for me?

Keeper's pricing page says yearly plans include filing a federal return plus up to 2 state returns, and the return is reviewed and signed by a tax pro. You still answer questions and review transactions in the app. TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium is software you fill out yourself; a human expert review costs more with Expert Assist Premium ($209 federal plus $69 per state).

I also sold stocks or have a rental. Which should I use?

Keeper lists rentals, K-1s and investors only on its Premium plan ($399 a year). TurboTax Do It Yourself Premium includes stock, crypto and rental reporting at $139 federal plus $64 per state. If you're comfortable doing your own return, TurboTax is cheaper for that mix.

Can I deduct what I pay for Keeper or TurboTax?

Partly. IRS Publication 334 says you can deduct on Schedule C the cost of preparing the part of your return that relates to your business as a sole proprietor. The share for your personal return isn't deductible there. Keep the receipt either way.
