Solo 401(k) vs SEP IRA for freelancers (2026)

By Freelance Ledger · Last reviewed October 17, 2026 · How we research

2026 limits from IRS Notice 2025-67 (as summarized by Ascensus, checked October 17, 2026). 2027 limits had not been officially announced when we checked. General information, not investment or tax advice.

Both accounts let a self-employed person save much more than an IRA allows, and the contributions are generally tax-deductible. The right one depends on your income and how much you want to save.

2026 limits at a glance

For the self-employed, the "20%" is of net earnings after deducting half of your self-employment tax (that is how the IRS's 25%-of-reduced-compensation rule works out).

Which should you choose?

  • Lower to middle profit, want to save more: the Solo 401(k) usually allows a bigger total because the employee deferral doesn't depend on a percentage of income. At $100,000 profit the maximum is roughly $43,000 in a Solo 401(k) versus roughly $18,600 in a SEP.
  • Very high profit: both reach the $72,000 cap, so simplicity may decide it and the SEP is easier.
  • Want it simple, or contributing late: a SEP can be opened and funded by your tax-filing deadline, including extensions.
  • Plan to hire employees: a SEP requires contributing the same percentage for eligible employees; a Solo 401(k) is only for owners (and spouses). Talk to a professional before hiring.

Deadlines for opening a plan and making employee deferrals have changed in recent years, so check IRS Publication 560 or your provider before assuming. Contribution limits are from the IRS's annual announcement; see the IRS page on 401(k) and profit-sharing contribution limits.

Other limits worth knowing

The 2026 traditional or Roth IRA limit is $7,500 (plus $1,100 if 50 or older) and the SIMPLE IRA limit is $17,000. Deductibility of IRA contributions and Roth eligibility depend on income.

FAQ

Can I have a Solo 401(k) and a day job 401(k)?

Yes, but the employee deferral limit ($24,500 in 2026) applies across all your 401(k)s combined. The employer portion from your own business is separate.

Does contributing lower my self-employment tax?

No. These contributions reduce income tax but not self-employment tax, which is based on net earnings from self-employment.

Which has lower fees?

It depends on the provider. Many brokerages offer both with no account fee. Compare the investment options and any plan fees.
