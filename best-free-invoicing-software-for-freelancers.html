Plenty of invoicing apps are free to start. What differs is where the free plan stops (clients, invoices a month, reminders, branding) and what it costs when a client actually pays you, because that is how free tools make their money. We compared seven free options on both, using each company’s own pricing pages on November 16, 2026. For paid apps such as FreshBooks and Bonsai, see our best invoicing apps guide.

Quick picks

  • Best overall free plan: Wave Starter. Unlimited invoices, estimates and clients, plus free bookkeeping records.
  • Cheapest to get paid by bank transfer: Zoho Invoice (free, up to 500 invoices a year) or Invoice Ninja (free for up to 5 clients), each connected to Stripe: ACH costs 0.8% capped at $5.
  • Best if clients like PayPal or Venmo: PayPal Invoicing. Free to send, and ACH is capped at $10.
  • Best if you also take payments in person: Square Free, but its online card rate is the highest here.
  • Only a few invoices a month: Indy Free (3 a month) or our free invoice generator (no account at all).

Three things stand out:

  • Card fees are close; ACH fees are not. On a $2,000 invoice the card fee ranges from $58.30 to $66.30, but ACH ranges from $5.00 to $20.00. If you bill 24 invoices of $2,000 a year and every client pays by ACH, that is $120 a year through Stripe, $240 through PayPal, and $480 through Wave, Square Free or QuickBooks. Paid by card, the same year costs $1,399.20 to $1,591.20.
  • Square’s free plan charges more for invoices. Square Free’s online/invoice card rate is 3.3% + 30¢, versus 2.9% + 30¢ on its paid Plus plan, and its ACH fee has no cap on Free (Plus caps it at $10).
  • Small invoices favor percentage-only pricing. On a $300 card payment QuickBooks’ flat 2.99% ($8.97) beats Wave’s 60-cent fixed fee ($9.30). Wave Pro waives the fixed fee on your first 10 card payments a month (2.9% + $0, so $58.00 on $2,000), then goes back to 2.9% + $0.60.

Easy win: ask clients on large invoices to pay by bank transfer, and say so in your payment terms before the work starts. Check each tool’s settings for which payment methods appear on an invoice.

The seven options in one paragraph each

1. Wave Starter: best overall free plan

Wave’s free plan has no invoice or client cap and keeps your bookkeeping in the same place, which is rare. The catch is that its smoother features (emailing invoices from Wave, deposits on estimates, automatic reminders) need online payments turned on, and Wave’s 60-cent fixed card fee is the highest here. Online payments require approval, including identity verification and a credit review. Upgrade to Pro ($190 a year) when you want bank transactions imported automatically. More detail: Wave vs QuickBooks, Wave vs FreshBooks and Wave alternatives.

2. Zoho Invoice: best free plan for low ACH fees

Zoho Invoice is free with no paid tier listed, and includes reminders, recurring invoices, quotes, time tracking and a client portal. The 500-invoices-a-year cap is plenty for most solos. It connects to Stripe, PayPal, Square, Authorize.net and other gateways and adds no fee of its own. Two cautions: invoices carry “Powered by Zoho Invoice”, and Zoho deletes data from accounts inactive for more than 180 days, so export a backup if you step away. If you later need full accounting, see Zoho Books vs FreshBooks.

3. Invoice Ninja Free: best for five clients or fewer

Invoice Ninja’s free plan is generous on features (recurring billing, auto-billing, deposits, time tracking, expenses) but caps you at 5 clients and leaves out automatic late-payment reminders. It supports Stripe, PayPal, Square and many other gateways, or plain bank-transfer instructions with no gateway fee. Pro is $140 a year if you pay annually.

4. Square Free: best if you also get paid in person

Square’s free plan includes a lot of invoicing for $0: recurring invoices, deposits, reminders, estimates and even contracts with e-signature. But its invoice card rate (3.3% + 30¢) is the most expensive here, and Square doesn’t support multi-currency. It makes most sense if you already use Square to take payments in person.

5. PayPal Invoicing: best if clients want to pay with PayPal or Venmo

PayPal Invoicing is free to send and your client doesn’t need a PayPal account. Card payments cost 2.99% + $0.49, PayPal and Venmo wallet payments 3.49% + $0.49, and Pay by Bank (ACH) 1% capped at $10. It has no bookkeeping, so pair it with a spreadsheet such as our expense tracker.

6. QuickBooks Free: only if you’ll use QuickBooks Payments

QuickBooks Free limits you to 2 invoices a month unless you qualify for and enable QuickBooks Payments, which then charges 2.99% on cards (including Amex, PayPal and Venmo) and 1% on ACH. It is a reasonable on-ramp if you expect to move to QuickBooks Lite ($20 a month after the intro discount) for mileage and contractors; otherwise Wave gives you more for free. See QuickBooks alternatives.

7. Indy Free: a few invoices plus contracts

Indy’s free plan allows 3 invoices, 3 contracts and 3 proposals a month for up to 3 clients, which suits a freelancer with one or two retainers. Pro is $300 for 2 years. For more contract tools, see best contract and proposal software.

When free stops being enough

  • You pass a cap: more than 5 clients on Invoice Ninja, 500 invoices a year on Zoho Invoice, 3 a month on Indy or 2 a month on QuickBooks Free without Payments.
  • You chase late payments by hand. Automatic reminders are free on Zoho Invoice and Square Free, need online payments on Wave Starter, and are a paid feature on Invoice Ninja. Our unpaid invoice follow-up plan has a schedule either way.
  • You want your own branding only. Wave, Zoho Invoice and Invoice Ninja all add their name on free plans.
  • You spend hours on bookkeeping. That is when a paid plan with bank imports (Wave Pro, QuickBooks Lite) or a full accounting app pays for itself. Compare them in best accounting software for freelancers.

Tax points

  • Report the full invoice, deduct the fee. If a client pays $2,000 and the processor keeps $58.30, your income is $2,000 and the fee is a business expense. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary and necessary to be deductible; processing fees and invoicing subscriptions for client work usually qualify.
  • Payment apps can send a Form 1099-K. The IRS 1099-K page says a payment app or online marketplace must send one if your payments for goods or services total over $20,000 in more than 200 transactions, but may send one for less. Clients who pay you directly may send a 1099-NEC; Publication 334 notes the reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025. Don’t count the same income twice: see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K. All income is taxable whether or not you get a form.
  • Keep your invoices. The IRS record-keeping page generally says to keep records for 3 years from the date you filed. Export invoices and payout reports at year end, especially from free plans that can delete inactive accounts.
  • None of these tools files your taxes or finds deductions for you. Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year (bookkeeping plus a federal and up to 2 state returns, reviewed and signed by a tax pro) and Bookkeeping Only is $20 a month, with a 14-day free trial (checked Nov 16, 2026).

Our take

Start with Wave Starter if you are in the US: no caps, bookkeeping included, and fees in line with the others on mid-size invoices. If your clients pay large invoices by bank transfer, Zoho Invoice plus Stripe is the cheapest way to collect ($5.00 on a $2,000 ACH payment versus $20.00 on Wave). Use PayPal only when a client insists on PayPal or Venmo, and Square Free only if you already take card payments in person. Whatever you pick, push big invoices toward ACH: over a year that matters more than which free plan you chose.

FAQ

What is the best free invoicing software for freelancers?

For most US freelancers, Wave's free Starter plan: unlimited invoices and estimates with no client cap, plus free bookkeeping records. If you want the cheapest bank-transfer fees, Zoho Invoice (free, up to 500 invoices a year) connected to Stripe costs 0.8% capped at $5 per ACH payment as of Nov 16, 2026. Invoice Ninja's free plan is good if you have five clients or fewer.

Is free invoicing software really free?

Sending invoices is free on all seven tools we compared, but getting paid online is not. Card payments cost about 2.9% to 3.3% plus a fixed fee on every tool, so a $2,000 invoice paid by card costs roughly $58 to $66 to collect. Bank transfers (ACH) are much cheaper: $5 to $20 on the same invoice, depending on the tool.

Which free invoice app has no limits on clients or invoices?

Wave Starter, Square Free and PayPal Invoicing don't cap invoices or clients on their free plans as of Nov 16, 2026. Zoho Invoice caps you at 500 invoices a year, Invoice Ninja's free plan at 5 clients, Indy's free plan at 3 invoices and 3 clients a month, and QuickBooks Free at 2 invoices a month unless you enable QuickBooks Payments.

Can I send an invoice without creating an account?

Yes. Our free invoice generator makes a printable invoice or PDF in your browser with no sign-up. You then email it yourself and the client pays you by bank transfer or check. You give up online card payments and automatic reminders, which is why most freelancers move to an app like Wave once they have regular clients.

Are invoicing app fees tax deductible?

Usually, if they are for your business. IRS Publication 334 says a deductible business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Payment processing fees and invoicing subscriptions for client work typically qualify. Report the full invoice amount as income, then deduct the fees, and keep your invoices and payout records; the IRS generally says to keep records for 3 years from when you filed.
