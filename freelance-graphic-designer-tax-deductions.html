Freelance graphic designer tax deductions (2026)

By Freelance Ledger · Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · How we research

Checked against IRS sources Oct 20, 2026: Publication 334, Publication 587, Publication 946 and Publication 463 (2025 editions, the latest the IRS has posted, plus their "What's New for 2026" notes), the 2025 Schedule C instructions, Topic 511, Topic 513 and the 2026 standard mileage rates page. Tax rules change. This is general information, not tax advice.

If you design for clients as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, your business expenses go on Schedule C. IRS Publication 334 sets the test: an expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate for your business). This is the designer cut of our general freelance tax deductions checklist; writers have their own writer version.

The short answer

Freelance designers usually write off design software and asset licenses (Adobe, Figma, fonts, stock images, mockups), computers, monitors and drawing tablets, a home office when the rules are met, the business share of phone and internet, portfolio and marketing costs, contractors you hire, courses that sharpen your current skills, and travel to clients or conferences. Business meals are generally 50% deductible and client gifts are capped at $25 per person per year. Commuting is not deductible.

Software, fonts and creative assets

  • Design software subscriptions: Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma, Sketch, Canva, Affinity, prototyping and color tools. Deduct the business-use share as a regular expense.
  • Licenses you buy for client work: fonts, stock photos and illustrations, icon sets, mockup and template packs, music for motion work. Keep the license receipt with the project it was used on.
  • Software bought outright (not a subscription): Publication 946 says off-the-shelf software can qualify for the Section 179 deduction; if you depreciate it instead, it is straight-line over 36 months.
  • Cloud storage, file transfer and project tools you use to deliver work.
  • Payment-processing and marketplace fees taken from what clients pay you.

Computers, tablets and other equipment

Hardware is where designers have the biggest single deductions, and you have three common routes. Whichever you use, deduct only the business-use percentage.

  • De minimis safe harbor: Publication 334 lets you elect to deduct tangible property costing up to $2,500 per item or invoice if you don't have an applicable financial statement (most freelancers don't). That often covers a drawing tablet, monitor, color calibrator, printer or mid-range laptop. It is an election you have to make; the IRS explains how at IRS.gov/Tangible-Property-Regulations.
  • Section 179: lets you expense qualifying equipment in the year you start using it. The dollar caps ($2,500,000 for tax years beginning in 2025 and $2,560,000 for 2026, per Publication 946) are far above what a solo designer spends; your deduction is also limited by your business income.
  • Depreciation / special depreciation allowance: computers and peripheral equipment are 5-year property. For qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, Publication 946 says the 100% special depreciation allowance (bonus depreciation) is back, so many purchases can still be written off in year one.

Cameras and video gear can be "listed property" with extra record rules unless used exclusively in your business (see the Line 13 notes in the Schedule C instructions). Keep a simple business-use log for anything you also use personally.

Marketing, portfolio and contractors

  • Portfolio website, domain and hosting, paid portfolio-platform memberships, and ads you run to get clients.
  • Printed samples, business cards and leave-behinds.
  • Professional dues and memberships tied to your design work.
  • Contractors you hire: illustrators, developers, copywriters, photographers, production artists. These go on Schedule C line 11 (contract labor). The 2025 instructions use a $600 Form 1099-NEC threshold; Publication 334 says the information-reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025. Collect a W-9 up front (see our W-9 guide).

Courses, conferences and travel

IRS Topic 513 allows self-employed people to deduct education that maintains or improves skills needed in their current work: a motion-design course or a Figma workshop for a working designer, for example. It does not cover education that qualifies you for a new trade or business. Tuition, books, supplies and certain travel to the course can count.

Overnight trips for clients or design conferences (airfare, lodging, 50% of qualifying meals) follow Topic 511 and Publication 463. Conventions held outside North America have stricter rules, and personal side trips are not deductible.

Home office

You need regular and exclusive use of part of your home as your principal place of business, or as a place where you meet clients (Publication 587). Publication 587's own example: a den the family also uses for recreation fails the exclusive-use test.

The simplified option is $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet ($1,500 max), with no depreciation to track. The regular method uses actual expenses (rent or mortgage interest, utilities, insurance) on Form 8829. Compare both in our home office deduction calculator.

Phone and internet

Deduct the business-use share of your cell phone and internet. The Schedule C instructions note one quirk: if you use a home landline for business, the base rate of the first phone line into your home isn't deductible, though extra business charges and a dedicated second line are.

Car and mileage

Driving to client meetings, print shops, photo shoots or supply runs counts; driving from home to a regular workplace is commuting. For 2026, the IRS business rates are 72.5¢/mile from Jan 1–Jun 30 and 76¢/mile from Jul 1–Dec 31 (2025: 70¢) per the IRS standard mileage rates page. Add business parking and tolls, or use actual expenses instead. Keep a mileage log; our mileage deduction calculator does the math.

Meals and client gifts

Business meals with a client or prospect are generally 50% deductible when they aren't lavish and you are present. Entertainment, such as concert or game tickets, is generally not deductible. Business gifts are limited to $25 per person per year (Publication 463), so a holiday gift box for a client will often be mostly nondeductible.

Creative property and capitalization

If you make and sell physical products, the IRS can require you to capitalize production costs. The Schedule C instructions say freelance artists, authors and photographers may be exempt from those uniform capitalization rules for property created by their own efforts, but not for printing and similar costs. Small business taxpayers have a separate exception. Most designers who sell services rather than prints won't need to deal with this. If you sell prints, merch or templates, ask a preparer how it applies to you.

Above-the-line items (not on Schedule C profit)

  • Half of self-employment tax as an adjustment to income. Estimate it with our self-employment tax calculator.
  • Self-employed health insurance premiums for you and your family, subject to profit and eligibility limits (Publication 334; Form 7206 in some cases).
  • Retirement contributions to a Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA: see Solo 401(k) vs SEP IRA.
  • Qualified business income (QBI) deduction: up to 20% of qualified business income; Publication 334 notes the 20% QBI deduction was made permanent. Our quarterly tax calculator applies it for simple cases.

Not deductible

Commuting, personal use of equipment or software, entertainment, fines and penalties, and spending on a hobby without a real profit motive. Repeated losses draw attention; Publication 334 covers profit motive and the excess-business-loss limits.

Records to keep

Keep receipts or bank records plus a one-line business purpose (client or project name works well for fonts and stock licenses). The IRS record-keeping page says to keep records for 3 years in most cases, longer in some situations. FAQ

Can I deduct Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma and font licenses?

Yes, when you use them for your design business. Subscriptions are deducted as a business expense on Schedule C. If you also use them personally, deduct only the business-use share.

Can I write off a new laptop or iPad all at once?

Often, yes. If you elect the de minimis safe harbor, you can deduct items costing up to $2,500 per item or invoice (no applicable financial statement). Pricier gear can usually be expensed with Section 179 or the special depreciation allowance instead of being depreciated over 5 years. Business-use share only; see Publication 946.

How much can I deduct for gifts to clients?

No more than $25 per person per year for business gifts, under IRS Publication 463. Incidental costs such as engraving, packaging or shipping generally do not count toward the $25.

What mileage rate do designers use in 2026?

For business miles, the IRS business standard mileage rates for 2026 are 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 through June 30, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 through December 31. Commuting does not count. Add business parking and tolls and keep a log.

Do I have to capitalize the costs of creating artwork?

The Schedule C instructions say freelance artists, authors and photographers may be exempt from the uniform capitalization rules for property their own efforts create. Printing and similar costs are not covered by that exception. Small business taxpayers also have an exception. If you sell prints or products, ask a tax professional.
