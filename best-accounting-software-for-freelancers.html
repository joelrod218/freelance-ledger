Most "best accounting software" lists are written for companies with staff, payroll and inventory. If you are a freelancer with no employees, you file a Schedule C, and your books need to do four things: record income, record expenses, send invoices (and ideally take payments), and hand you or your accountant a clean profit number at tax time. Payroll, inventory, purchase orders and multi-user permissions are features you pay for and never use.

We ranked the options below on that solo-freelancer job: real cost over a year, what the cheapest useful plan actually includes, and where each one stops fitting.

What a solo freelancer actually needs

  • Income and expense tracking that matches your bank. IRS Publication 583 says you can use any record-keeping system that clearly shows your income and expenses, and recommends reconciling your business account every month. Automatic bank import makes that fast; it is the feature most worth paying for.
  • Invoices and online payments, if clients pay you directly. Platform and agency work often doesn't need invoicing at all.
  • A profit and loss report that maps to Schedule C categories.
  • Receipt capture, so you can prove expenses later.
  • Accountant access, only if you use an accountant.

You don't need payroll, inventory or extra users. If a plan's main upgrade is one of those, skip it.

1. Wave: best free accounting for most US freelancers

Wave's Starter plan is $0 and includes unlimited estimates, invoices, bills and bookkeeping records, a mobile app, and optional online payments at 2.9% + $0.60 per card transaction (3.4% + $0.60 for Amex). The catch: on Starter you enter or upload transactions yourself. Automatic bank import, auto-categorization, unlimited receipt capture and automatic late-payment reminders are on Pro, at $19 a month or $190 a year. Pro also cuts the card fee to 2.9% + $0 for your first 10 transactions each month. Wave says the listed Pro price applies to new subscriptions.

Where it stops fitting: live support is chat and email on weekdays only, and add-ons such as bookkeeping help start at $149 a month. Verdict: start here if you want free books and you invoice clients directly. Pay for Pro once typing in transactions takes more than an hour a month.

2. Zoho Books: the most complete free plan

Zoho Books' Free plan is $0 for as long as your revenue for the financial year stays under $50K. It covers 1 user plus 1 accountant, up to 1,000 invoices and 1,000 expenses a year, 50 receipt autoscans a month, online payments, recurring invoices, payment reminders, bank reconciliation, mileage tracking, 1099 contractor tracking and P&L and balance sheet reports. Connecting automatic bank feeds is listed under Standard: $20 a month, or $15 a month billed annually ($180 a year), which also adds sales-tax tracking and 1099 e-filing. Zoho offers a 14-day free trial of paid plans.

Where it stops fitting: the free plan ends once revenue passes $50K, and the menus are busier than Wave's. Verdict: the best free option if you want an accountant to log in and you are under $50K. If you already use Zoho Mail or Zoho Invoice, it's an easy add.

3. QuickBooks Solopreneur: built for a business of one

Intuit describes QuickBooks Solopreneur as built for single-owner, self-employed businesses. The products page lists it at $20 a month, $10 a month for the first 3 months for new subscribers (Intuit's plan cards also call this plan "Lite"). It includes unlimited invoices, receipts and GPS mileage tracking, up to 3 contractors, payments by card, ACH, Apple Pay, PayPal and Venmo, and instant deposit on eligible payments. Tax filing is separate: Expert Assisted Tax powered by TurboTax, paid when you file, with 20% off the base price of federal filing.

The QuickBooks Free plan is $0 with no credit card, but it is limited to 2 invoices a month (unless you enable QuickBooks Payments), 1 bank connection and no accountant access. QuickBooks Online Simple Start is $38 a month list ($19 for 3 months) and adds accountant access and unlimited contractors.

Where it stops fitting: after the intro period it is $240 a year, which is more than Wave Pro or Zoho Standard billed annually. Verdict: the best pick if you drive a lot for work, your accountant works in QuickBooks, or you want the option to move up to QuickBooks Online later. 4. FreshBooks: best for billing time and projects

FreshBooks lists Lite at $23 a month (invoices to 5 clients), Plus at $43 (50 clients) and Premium at $70 (unlimited clients). On Oct 27, 2026 it showed 80% off for 4 months, so Lite was $4.60 a month during the promo. Card payments are 2.9% + $0.30 and ACH bank transfers are 1%. On the plan cards, accounting reports, receipt scanning and accountant access are listed from Plus, not Lite. Extra team members are $11 a month each. FreshBooks offers a 30-day free trial with no credit card.

Where it stops fitting: the client caps. A freelancer with more than 5 clients a year needs Plus, which is $516 a year at list price. Verdict: the friendliest tool for time tracking, proposals and polished invoices. Budget for the list price, not the promo. See Wave vs FreshBooks.

5. Xero: best if your accountant uses it

Xero's US Early plan lists at $27 a month and includes quotes, 20 invoices, 5 bills, bank reconciliation, document capture, W-9 and 1099 management, and sales tax. New US customers get 90% off for the first 6 months ($2.70 a month) on offers through Nov 2, 2026. Growing ($59) removes the invoice and bill limits. Auto-reconcile is a $5-a-month add-on on Early.

Where it stops fitting: 20 invoices a month is fine for most freelancers but not for high-volume billing, and the jump to Growing is large. Verdict: clean, accountant-friendly books. Choose it when your accountant asks for Xero; otherwise Wave or Zoho cost less for the same solo job.

6. Bonsai: client workflow first, accounting second

Bonsai Essentials ($25 a month, or $19 a month billed annually) includes invoices and payments, proposals and contracts, a client portal, and expense and income tracking. Integrations with QuickBooks and Xero start on Premium ($39 a month, or $29 billed annually). There is a 7-day free trial.

Verdict: not a replacement for accounting software, but a strong front end if contracts and proposals are part of how you sell. Many freelancers pair it with Wave or QuickBooks.

7. Keeper: if you mostly want taxes handled

Keeper (affiliate link) isn't accounting software in the usual sense: there are no invoices or balance sheet. It connects unlimited bank and card accounts, finds and categorizes deductions, and its yearly plans include filing. Standard is $199 a year with a federal and up to 2 state returns reviewed and signed by a tax pro; Bookkeeping Only (no filing) is $20 a month; every plan has a 14-day free trial (pricing page, Oct 27, 2026).

Verdict: a good fit for platform, agency and other 1099 work where clients don't need invoices from you. If you do invoice, pair it with Wave Starter or QuickBooks Free.

How to choose in five minutes

  • Under $50K revenue and want $0: Zoho Books Free, or Wave Starter if you don't mind entering transactions.
  • Want automatic bank import for the least money: Zoho Books Standard billed annually ($180) or Wave Pro ($190).
  • Drive for work or your accountant uses QuickBooks: QuickBooks Solopreneur.
  • Bill by the hour or by project: FreshBooks (plan around the 5-client cap on Lite).
  • Accountant uses Xero: Xero Early.
  • Mostly 1099 platform income, rarely invoice: Keeper (affiliate link), or our free expense tracker spreadsheet.

Record-keeping rules that apply whichever tool you pick

  • Same method for books and taxes. Publication 583 says you choose an accounting method (most freelancers use the cash method: income when received, expenses when paid) when you file your first return, and you must use the same method to keep your books.
  • Use a separate business account. Publication 583 recommends opening a business checking account, keeping it separate from personal money and reconciling it each month. Software is much easier to keep clean when only business transactions flow through it. See business bank accounts for freelancers.
  • Keep the source documents. Invoices, receipts and account statements support your entries. Proof of payment alone does not prove an expense is deductible, so keep the receipt or invoice too.
  • Digital records count. Publication 583 says a computerized system must be able to produce legible records that reconcile with your books and your return.
  • Keep records at least 3 years after filing in most cases, 6 years if income was underreported by more than 25%, and records for equipment until the period of limitations runs out for the year you sell or dispose of it. Export your data every year, so cancelling a subscription never costs you your records.

Our take

For most freelancers with no employees, the best accounting software is the cheapest one that imports your bank transactions automatically, because that is what keeps the books current. Today that means Zoho Books (free under $50K revenue, or $180 a year for bank feeds) or Wave Pro ($190 a year). Choose QuickBooks Solopreneur when mileage tracking or your accountant's preference matters, and FreshBooks when you bill time for a handful of clients. If what you really want is taxes done, Keeper (affiliate link) covers the deductions and the return for $199 a year.

FAQ

Do I need accounting software if I'm a freelancer with no employees?

Not legally. IRS Publication 583 says that, except in a few cases, the law doesn't require any specific kind of records: you can use any system that clearly shows your income and expenses. A spreadsheet works at low volume if you reconcile it to your bank statements. Software starts to pay for itself when you send many invoices, take card payments, have dozens of transactions a month, or work with an accountant.

What is the best free accounting software for a solo freelancer?

As of Oct 27, 2026: Wave's Starter plan is $0 with unlimited invoices and bookkeeping records (automatic bank import needs Pro at $19 a month), and Zoho Books' Free plan is $0 for businesses with under $50K in revenue for the financial year, for 1 user plus 1 accountant and up to 1,000 invoices a year. QuickBooks also has a Free plan, but it is limited to 2 invoices a month.

Do I need double-entry bookkeeping?

Not as a sole proprietor. IRS Publication 583 calls single-entry the simplest system and says it can be practical for a small business; double-entry has built-in checks that catch errors. Accounting apps handle that structure for you (FreshBooks, for example, lists double-entry accounting reports on its pricing page), so you don't have to write debits and credits yourself.

Is QuickBooks Solopreneur enough for a freelancer?

For a one-person Schedule C business, often yes. Intuit describes it as built for single-owner, self-employed businesses: income and expense tracking, unlimited invoices, receipts and GPS mileage, and up to 3 contractors on the paid plan. It lists at $20 a month ($10 a month for the first 3 months for new subscribers as of Oct 27, 2026). Move up to QuickBooks Online if you need more contractors, accountant access on the plan, payroll or inventory.

Is accounting software tax-deductible?

Generally yes, if you use it for your business. IRS Publication 583 says a deductible business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Subscription software you use to run the business usually meets that test; split the cost if you also use it personally.
