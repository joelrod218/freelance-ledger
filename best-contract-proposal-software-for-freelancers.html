A freelancer’s contract software has three jobs: send a proposal the client can say yes to, get the contract signed electronically, and collect the deposit. Some tools do all three in one link; others only handle the signing. We compared six that solo freelancers actually use, on the plan that first includes contracts or proposals, with prices as of Nov 13, 2026.

The short answer

  • Best value all-in-one: Bonsai Essentials, $19 a month billed annually ($228 a year). Proposals, contracts, all templates, invoices and payments, time tracking and expense tracking.
  • Best for booking clients from inquiries: HoneyBook Starter, $29 a month billed annually. Proposals and contracts with e-signatures on every plan, free setup of your existing contracts as templates, 30-day trial.
  • Best for automated onboarding and cheap ACH: Dubsado, $335 a year for Starter. Contracts with time-stamped e-signatures; ACH through Dubsado Payments is 0.8% capped at $5.
  • Best if you already invoice in FreshBooks: FreshBooks Plus. Proposals and e-signatures start on Plus; no separate contract template library is listed.
  • Best for signing only, free: PandaDoc Free (5 documents a month) or Indy Free (3 contracts, 3 proposals and 3 invoices a month, up to 3 clients).

What to put in a freelance contract

The software only sends what you write. Most freelance contract templates cover the same ground; check that yours does before you send it:

  • Scope and deliverables: what you will deliver, in what format, and what is out of scope.
  • Timeline: start date, milestones, and what happens if the client is late with feedback or materials.
  • Price and payment terms: total or rate, deposit, due dates (for example net 15), accepted payment methods, and any late fee. Our unpaid invoice follow-up plan covers what to do when a payment slips.
  • Revisions and change requests: how many rounds are included and how extra work is billed.
  • Ownership and usage rights: when rights to the work transfer (often on final payment) and whether you can show it in your portfolio.
  • Cancellation: notice, a kill fee or non-refundable deposit, and payment for work done to date.
  • Confidentiality and how disputes are handled.

On e-signatures: the federal ESIGN Act, 15 U.S.C. 7001 says a signature or contract in interstate commerce may not be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic. It also sets extra consent rules when a law requires disclosures to a consumer in writing, and state law still governs whether a contract’s terms hold up. Whatever tool you use, download the signed copy and keep it with your records.

Tax points

  • The subscription is usually deductible. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Contract software and payment processing fees for client work typically qualify.
  • Keep signed contracts with your records. The IRS record-keeping page generally says to keep records for 3 years from the date you filed (longer in some situations). A signed contract plus the invoice and payment record backs up your income and deposits.
  • Clients may ask for a W-9 before the first payment. See our W-9 guide for freelancers. Publication 334 notes the 1099 information-reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025, but all income is taxable either way.
  • None of these tools files your taxes. If you want write-offs found and a return filed, Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year (federal plus up to 2 state returns) and Bookkeeping Only is $20 a month, with a 14-day free trial (checked Nov 13, 2026).

Our take

For most solo freelancers, Bonsai Essentials on annual billing is the best value: contracts, proposals, invoices and payments for $228 a year. Choose HoneyBook if your work starts with inquiries and you want a polished booking flow, or Dubsado if you want deep automations and the cheapest ACH on large deposits. If you only need a few signatures a month, start free with Indy or PandaDoc before paying for anything.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to send freelance contracts with e-signatures?

Free plans exist. As of Nov 13, 2026, Indy's free plan allows 3 proposals, 3 contracts and 3 invoices a month for up to 3 clients, and PandaDoc's free plan allows 5 documents a month with e-signatures. If you need more, the cheapest paid options we found are Indy Pro ($25 a month, or $300 for 2 years), PandaDoc Starter ($19 a month billed annually) and Bonsai Essentials ($19 a month billed annually), which also includes invoicing and payments.

Are e-signed contracts legally binding?

In the US, the federal ESIGN Act (15 U.S.C. 7001) says a contract or signature in interstate commerce can't be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic. That doesn't make every contract enforceable: the terms, the parties and state law still matter, and the law has conditions and exceptions. This is general information, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review your template if a lot of money is at stake.

Do I need a separate contract app if I already use FreshBooks?

Maybe not for proposals. FreshBooks lists proposals and e-signatures from its Plus plan ($43 a month list price as of Nov 13, 2026), and estimates on every plan, but its pricing page doesn't list contract templates. If you want a full contract template library and a sign-then-pay flow, Bonsai, HoneyBook and Dubsado build that in.

Which tool has the lowest payment fees for big invoices?

For bank transfers (ACH), Dubsado Payments charges 0.8% capped at $5, so a $2,000 ACH payment costs $5. Bonsai and FreshBooks charge 1% ($20 on $2,000) and HoneyBook charges 1.5% ($30). Card fees are close on all four at about 2.9% plus 25 to 30 cents, but FreshBooks charges 3.5% + $0.30 for American Express and business cards.

Is contract software tax deductible?

Usually, if you use it for your business. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Software subscriptions and payment processing fees for client work typically qualify. Keep the receipts and signed contracts with your records; the IRS generally says to keep records for 3 years from when you filed.
