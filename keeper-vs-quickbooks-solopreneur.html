Keeper vs QuickBooks Solopreneur (2026)

Keeper and QuickBooks Solopreneur both promise to sort a one-person business's transactions and find deductions, so they get compared a lot. They are built for different jobs. Keeper is a deduction tracker plus tax filing, with returns reviewed and signed by a tax pro. QuickBooks Solopreneur is bookkeeping software with invoicing, payments and mileage tracking; tax filing is a separate TurboTax-powered service you pay for when you file.

The short answer

  • Pick Keeper if your clients pay you without invoices from you (platforms, agencies, 1099 work), your main worry is missing write-offs and you want your return prepared and filed for one yearly price.
  • Pick QuickBooks Solopreneur if you send a lot of invoices, want clients to pay by card, ACH, PayPal or Venmo, track mileage with your phone's GPS, or pay a few 1099 contractors, and you are happy to file your taxes separately.
  • Use both (QuickBooks Free for invoices, Keeper for taxes) if you only send a couple of invoices a month and want filing included. See the FAQ.

Cost over a year

  • Keeper Standard: $199 for the year, tax filing included.
  • Keeper Bookkeeping Only: $240 for 12 months ($20 × 12), no filing. Keeper itself points out that Standard costs less.
  • QuickBooks Solopreneur: $210 in year one (3 × $10 + 9 × $20), then $240 a year, plus whatever TurboTax-powered filing costs when you file.
  • QuickBooks Free + Keeper Standard: $199 for the year if 2 invoices a month is enough.

So on software price alone they are close. The real difference is that Keeper's price includes a prepared and filed return, and QuickBooks' price includes invoicing and payments. Card and payment processing fees on QuickBooks invoices are extra and usually cost more than the subscription if you bill a lot by card.

Who should pick which

  • Rideshare, delivery, platform or agency 1099 work: Keeper. You rarely invoice, and deductions plus filing are the whole job.
  • Designer, developer or consultant who bills clients directly: QuickBooks Solopreneur for invoicing and payments; add Keeper (or a preparer) for filing if you want a pro-reviewed return.
  • Lots of driving: QuickBooks' GPS mileage tracking is available now; Keeper lists mileage as coming soon. Our mileage deduction calculator shows what the miles are worth.
  • W-2 job plus a side business, or rentals, K-1s, 3+ states: Keeper Premium covers those on one return.
  • You've elected S-corp status: Keeper Business, or a full accounting tool with payroll (QuickBooks Online plus payroll) and an accountant.
  • Budget of $0: QuickBooks Free or our free expense tracker template, then file however you file now.

Tax points that apply either way

  • The software is a business expense. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Bookkeeping and tax-prep software for your business usually qualifies; split it if you also use it for personal taxes.
  • Estimated taxes don't go away. The IRS estimated taxes page says you generally must pay during the year if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file. Neither tool sends payments for you on its basic plan; our tax set-aside calculator and quarterly estimated taxes guide cover how much and when.
  • 1099 thresholds changed. Publication 334 says the information-reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025, so you may get fewer 1099s. You still report all your income; tracking it in either tool helps (see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K).
  • Keep records. The IRS record-keeping page says to keep records for 3 years in most cases. Export your data each year whichever app you use, so you still have it if you cancel.

Our take

For most solo freelancers who mainly want taxes handled, Keeper (affiliate link) Standard at $199 a year is the simpler buy: the deduction finding and a pro-reviewed return come in one price. If you run your business on invoices and online payments, QuickBooks Solopreneur does more day to day, and at $20 a month after the intro period it's priced about the same as Keeper's bookkeeping tier. Trying Keeper's 14-day trial and QuickBooks' Free plan side by side costs nothing.

FAQ

Is Keeper or QuickBooks Solopreneur cheaper?

It depends on whether you count tax filing. As of Oct 26, 2026, Keeper Standard is $199 a year and includes a federal return and up to 2 state returns. QuickBooks Solopreneur lists at $20 a month ($10 a month for the first 3 months for new subscribers), so about $210 in year one and $240 a year after that, and tax filing through its TurboTax-powered Expert Assisted Tax is paid separately when you file.

Can QuickBooks Solopreneur file my taxes?

Not on its own. Intuit's solopreneur page says the paid plan includes access to Expert Assisted Tax powered by TurboTax, where you pay only when you file and get 20% off the base price of federal filing. Keeper's yearly plans include filing in the subscription price.

Does Keeper do invoicing?

Keeper's pricing page lists bookkeeping (expense tracking, deduction finding, receipts, income tracking) and tax filing, but not invoicing or card payments. If you need to send invoices and take payments, QuickBooks Solopreneur (or a separate invoicing app) covers that.

Is QuickBooks Solopreneur the same as QuickBooks Lite?

Intuit's pages use both names right now. The products page lists QuickBooks Solopreneur at $20 a month ($10 for the first 3 months), and the solopreneur and self-employed pages show the same plan card as Lite with the same price. A QuickBooks team reply in Intuit's community says an existing Solopreneur subscription can't be switched directly to Lite, so existing customers should check with Intuit before changing.

Can I use both Keeper and QuickBooks?

Yes. A common low-cost setup is the QuickBooks Free plan ($0, up to 2 invoices a month, 1 bank connection) for invoicing and Keeper for deductions and filing. If you invoice a lot, the paid QuickBooks plan adds unlimited invoices. Just make sure income isn't counted twice when you file.
