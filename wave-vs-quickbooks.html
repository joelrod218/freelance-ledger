Wave vs QuickBooks for Freelancers (2026)

By Freelance Ledger · Published November 10, 2026 · How we research

Wave and QuickBooks are the two names most freelancers hit first when they look for bookkeeping software, and both now have a $0 plan. The free plans are built very differently, though. Wave Starter gives you unlimited invoices and bookkeeping records but leaves bank imports, receipt capture and emailed reminders for its paid Pro plan or its payments add-on. QuickBooks Free connects a bank and tracks mileage but caps you at 2 invoices a month unless you turn on QuickBooks Payments. Here is how they compare for a one-person business in the US.

The short answer

  • Pick Wave if you want the lowest software bill: Starter is $0 with unlimited invoices, and Pro is $190 a year for bank auto-import, receipt capture and automatic reminders. Your business must be in the US or Canada.
  • Pick QuickBooks (Lite, $20 a month list) if you drive for work and want GPS mileage tracking, pay a few 1099 contractors, want clients to pay by PayPal or Venmo, or plan to have an accountant or TurboTax-powered expert do your return from the same data.
  • Card fees are close (2.9% + $0.60 on Wave Starter vs 2.99% on QuickBooks). Wave Pro is cheapest for your first 10 card payments each month. ACH is 1% on both.

Who should pick which

  • New freelancer, few clients, $0 budget: Wave Starter. Unlimited invoices beat QuickBooks Free’s 2 a month, unless you plan to take every payment through QuickBooks Payments anyway.
  • You want bank feeds and receipts without a big bill: Wave Pro at $190 a year.
  • You drive for work: QuickBooks Lite for GPS mileage. Our mileage deduction calculator shows what the miles are worth, and our mileage apps comparison covers standalone trackers if you stay on Wave.
  • You pay subcontractors: QuickBooks, which tracks contractor payments and prepares 1099s (3 contractors on Lite). See our W-9 guide.
  • Clients want to pay by PayPal or Venmo: QuickBooks lists both; Wave lists cards, bank payments and Apple Pay.
  • Outside the US or Canada: not Wave. Wave’s own help center points users outside those countries to Zoho Books.
  • You bill by the hour and want time tracking: neither plan lists it; look at FreshBooks instead.

Tax points that apply either way

  • The subscription is usually deductible. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Bookkeeping software and payment processing fees for your business generally qualify; see our freelance tax deductions checklist.
  • Neither app files your return by itself. Both produce the profit and loss report you need for Schedule C. If you want deductions found and a return prepared and filed for one yearly price, Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year (federal plus up to 2 state returns, reviewed and signed by a tax pro), and Bookkeeping Only is $20 a month, with a 14-day free trial (prices as of Nov 10, 2026). See Keeper vs QuickBooks Solopreneur.
  • Pay estimated taxes on what clients pay you. The IRS estimated taxes page says sole proprietors generally must make estimated payments if they expect to owe $1,000 or more when they file. Our tax set-aside calculator shows how much of each payment to move aside.
  • Card payments can show up on a 1099-K. Payments you take through either processor may be reported to the IRS on Form 1099-K, and Publication 334 says the 1099-NEC reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025. Report all your income either way (see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K).
  • Keep your records if you switch. The IRS record-keeping page says to keep records for 3 years in most cases. Export your reports and transactions before you cancel either app.

Our take

For most solo freelancers in the US who mainly send invoices, Wave is the better value: Starter costs nothing with unlimited invoices, and Pro at $190 a year adds bank feeds, receipts and reminders for less than QuickBooks Lite. QuickBooks Lite earns its $20 a month if you track a lot of business miles, pay contractors, need PayPal or Venmo on invoices, or want your books in the system most accountants already use. Both have a $0 plan, so run one month of real invoices through each before you pay for anything.

FAQ

Is Wave or QuickBooks cheaper for a freelancer?

Wave, on subscription price. As of Nov 10, 2026, Wave Starter is $0 and Wave Pro is $190 a year billed annually ($19 a month billed monthly). QuickBooks Free is $0 but limited to 2 invoices a month unless you enable QuickBooks Payments; the solo plan, Lite (also called Solopreneur), lists at $20 a month with 50% off the first 3 months for new subscribers, so about $210 in year one and $240 after that. Card processing fees usually cost more than either subscription, so compare those too.

Does QuickBooks Free really let me send unlimited invoices?

Intuit's plan card says QuickBooks Free lets you send up to two invoices a month, or unlimited invoices if you qualify for and enable QuickBooks Payments. The Free plan also has no mobile app, no third-party integrations, 1 connected bank, 2 receipt uploads and 5 mileage trips a month.

Which has lower card fees, Wave or QuickBooks?

Close. Wave charges 2.9% + $0.60 per Visa, Mastercard or Discover payment on Starter (3.4% + $0.60 for Amex); Wave Pro drops the 60 cents on your first 10 card payments each month. QuickBooks Payments lists 2.99% for invoice card payments with no fixed fee. On a $2,000 invoice that is $58.60 on Wave Starter, $58.00 on Wave Pro (within the first 10) and $59.80 on QuickBooks. ACH bank payments are 1% on both; Wave has a $1 minimum.

Can I use Wave if I live outside the US or Canada?

Not for new businesses. Wave's help center says it is available for businesses located in the United States and Canada (US territories are not supported), and that support for users outside those countries was discontinued after Wave's November 2020 decision to focus on the US and Canada.

Does either one file my taxes?

No. Both give you a profit and loss report for your Schedule C. QuickBooks Lite includes access to Expert Assisted Tax powered by TurboTax, which you pay for only when you file (Intuit lists 20% off the base price of federal filing). If you want deductions found and a return prepared for one yearly price, Keeper Standard is $199 a year with a 14-day free trial (as of Nov 10, 2026).
