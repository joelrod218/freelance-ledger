Same loan, different terms

Why total cost matters more than the monthly payment

The CFPB and the FTC both say to compare the total cost of a loan, not just the monthly payment. A longer term usually means a lower payment but more total interest, and it also keeps you at risk of owing more than the car is worth for longer (negative equity). The CFPB's sample for a $20,000 loan at 4.75% shows the effect: about $1,498 of interest over 36 months versus about $3,024 over 72 months.

The CFPB's definition of "amount financed" includes the vehicle price, taxes and government fees, and any add-ons, minus your down payment and trade-in. That's what this calculator uses. Taxes, title and registration fees aren't negotiable, but dealer fees and add-ons often are.

If your income varies

Pick a payment you could cover in a slow month, not an average one, and remember insurance, fuel, registration and repairs come on top of the loan. Our guide on getting a car loan with variable income covers the application, preapproval and negotiating steps. Owning a car also changes your taxes if you use it for work, so see our estimated taxes guide, and if you're also planning a home purchase, try the mortgage calculator, since a car payment adds to your monthly debt on a mortgage application.

Limits of this calculator

  • It assumes a fixed-rate, monthly-payment loan with no prepayment penalty and no payments deferred.
  • Lenders' APR figures can include certain fees, and your actual payment can differ slightly from this estimate.
  • It doesn't show whether you'll qualify or what rate you'll get; compare offers from several lenders.
