Freelance writer tax deductions (2026)

By Freelance Ledger · Last reviewed October 19, 2026 · How we research

Rules checked against IRS Publication 334, the IRS home-office and simplified-option pages, Topic 511 (travel), and the 2026 standard mileage rates page on October 19, 2026. Tax rules change. This is general information, not tax advice.

If you write for clients as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, your deductible expenses go on Schedule C. A cost is deductible when it is ordinary (common for writers) and necessary (helpful for the business). That standard is in IRS Publication 334 (Tax Guide for Small Business). This page is the writer-focused cut of our general freelance tax deductions checklist.

The short answer

Writers usually write off software and research tools, a home office or coworking when the rules are met, business phone and internet share, professional dues, marketing, contractor payments (editors, designers), and travel to client meetings or conferences. Meals are generally 50% deductible. Commuting and everyday clothes are not.

Everyday costs for writers

  • Writing and research software (word processors, Scrivener-style apps, grammar tools, Adobe, transcription) used for client work.
  • Website, domain, hosting and newsletter tools you use to get or serve clients.
  • Professional dues and memberships tied to writing work.
  • Education that maintains or improves skills for your current writing business (not training for a new career).
  • Payment-processing and platform fees you pay to get paid.
  • Phone and internet: the business-use percentage only.
  • Equipment: laptop, monitor, microphone, chair. Small items are often expensed; larger purchases may use depreciation or Section 179—see Publication 946 and your preparer.
  • Books and subscriptions bought for assignments or trade research, not personal reading.
  • Contractors: editors, proofreaders, designers you hire. For 2026, the information-reporting threshold for certain Forms 1099 rose to $2,000 (see the 2026 Schedule C instructions); confirm what you must file.

Home office

You need regular and exclusive use of part of your home as your principal place of business (or a place where you meet clients). The IRS explains this on its home office deduction page and in Publication 587.

The simplified option is $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet ($1,500 max), with no depreciation. Or use the regular method with actual expenses and Form 8829. Try our home office calculator.

Working at a coffee shop does not create a home-office deduction for that café. A café purchase is usually personal unless it is a qualifying business meal.

Car, mileage and travel

Business miles (client meetings, research trips, conferences—not commuting) can use the standard mileage rate or actual expenses. For 2026, the IRS business rates are 72.5¢/mile from Jan 1–Jun 30 and 76¢/mile from Jul 1–Dec 31 (IRS standard mileage rates). Add parking and tolls. Keep a contemporaneous log. Use our mileage calculator.

Overnight business travel (airfare, lodging, 50% of qualifying meals) follows Topic 511 and Publication 463. Personal side trips are not deductible.

Meals

Business meals are generally 50% deductible when ordinary, necessary, and not lavish. Keep the receipt and note who and why. Entertainment is generally not deductible.

Above-the-line items (not on Schedule C profit)

  • Half of self-employment tax as an adjustment to income.
  • Self-employed health insurance premiums, subject to profit and eligibility limits (see Form 7206 / Publication 334).
  • Retirement contributions to a Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA: see Solo 401(k) vs SEP IRA.
  • Qualified business income (QBI) deduction: up to 20% of qualified business income for eligible businesses; Pub 334 notes the 20% QBI deduction was made permanent. Our quarterly tax calculator applies it for simple cases.

Not deductible

Commuting, everyday clothing suitable for street wear, personal entertainment, fines and penalties, and expenses for a hobby without a real profit motive. If you show losses year after year, talk to a tax professional—Publication 334 covers profit motive and excess-business-loss limits.

Records to keep

Receipts or bank records, plus a short note of the business purpose. Keep tax records at least three years after filing (longer in some cases). A simple spreadsheet works: use our free expense tracker.

FAQ

Can I deduct Scrivener, Grammarly or Adobe?

Yes, if you use the subscription for writing work. Deduct the business-use share on Schedule C.

Can I deduct a coffee shop as my office?

Buying a coffee while you work is usually a personal expense. A home office needs regular and exclusive use of a space in your home. See Publication 587 and the IRS simplified home-office page.

Can I deduct books and magazine subscriptions?

Yes when they are ordinary and necessary for your writing business (research for an assignment, trade publications). Personal pleasure reading is not deductible.

What mileage rate do writers use in 2026?

For business miles, the IRS business standard mileage rates for 2026 are 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 through June 30, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 through December 31. Add parking and tolls. Keep a log.
