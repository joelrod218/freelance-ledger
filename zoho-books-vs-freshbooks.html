Zoho Books vs FreshBooks for Freelancers (2026)

Zoho Books and FreshBooks both handle invoices, expenses and online payments for a one-person business, and both run reports you can hand to whoever does your taxes. They price very differently, though. Zoho Books has a $0 plan for businesses under $50K in yearly revenue and cheap paid tiers, but time tracking only starts on its Professional plan. FreshBooks costs more and caps clients by plan, but every plan includes time tracking, automatic bank import and scheduled late fees. Here is how they compare for a US freelancer.

The short answer

  • Pick Zoho Books if you want the lowest bill: Free is $0 under $50K revenue (with an accountant login included), and Standard is $180 a year billed annually for bank feeds. It also wins on bank-transfer fees (0.8%, capped at $5) and if you have lots of small clients, since it limits invoices per year rather than clients.
  • Pick FreshBooks if you bill by the hour or by project and want time tracking, mileage, automatic bank import and late fees on the cheapest plan, with a simpler, invoicing-first app. Lite ($23 a month list) works if you have 5 or fewer clients; Plus ($43) covers 50.
  • Card fees are the same for standard US cards (2.9% + $0.30). FreshBooks charges more for Amex and business cards (3.5% + $0.30) and for ACH on large invoices (1%, uncapped).

Who should pick which

  • New freelancer under $50K, $0 budget: Zoho Books Free. It does more than most free plans (accountant login, recurring invoices, reminders, mileage, 1099 prep). Compare it with Wave and QuickBooks Free.
  • You bill hourly or by project: FreshBooks Lite (5 clients or fewer) or Plus. Time tracking is included; on Zoho it means Professional at $480 a year. Our hourly rate calculator helps you set the rate.
  • Clients pay big invoices by bank transfer: Zoho, for the $5 ACH cap.
  • Clients pay with company cards or Amex: Zoho’s pricing page shows one domestic card rate; FreshBooks charges 3.5% + $0.30 on those cards.
  • Many small clients: Zoho, which limits invoices per year rather than clients; FreshBooks would push you to Plus or Premium.
  • You pay subcontractors: Zoho tracks 1099 contractors and prepares 1099s even on Free. See our W-9 guide.
  • You want the simplest invoicing app: FreshBooks, which is built around invoices and time; Zoho Books is a fuller accounting system with busier menus.

Tax points that apply either way

  • The subscription is usually deductible. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Accounting software and payment processing fees for your business generally qualify; see our freelance tax deductions checklist.
  • Neither app files your return. Both produce the profit and loss report you need for Schedule C. If you want deductions found and a return prepared and filed for one yearly price, Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year (federal plus up to 2 state returns, reviewed and signed by a tax pro), and Bookkeeping Only is $20 a month, with a 14-day free trial (prices as of Nov 11, 2026). See Keeper vs QuickBooks Solopreneur.
  • Pay estimated taxes on what clients pay you. The IRS estimated taxes page says sole proprietors generally must make estimated payments if they expect to owe $1,000 or more when they file. Our tax set-aside calculator shows how much of each payment to move aside.
  • 1099s you send and receive. Publication 334 says the 1099-NEC reporting threshold rises to $2,000 for payments made after 2025. Payments you take by card through either processor may also be reported on Form 1099-K. Report all your income either way (see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K).
  • Keep your records if you switch. The IRS record-keeping page says to keep records for 3 years in most cases. Export reports and transactions before you cancel either app.

Our take

For most solo freelancers watching costs, Zoho Books is the better value: the Free plan covers a real bookkeeping setup under $50K in revenue, Standard adds bank feeds for $180 a year, and its capped ACH fee saves real money on large invoices. FreshBooks earns its higher price if you track time, want bank import, mileage and late fees on the entry plan, or just prefer a simpler invoicing-first app; budget for Plus ($43 a month) once you pass 5 clients. Zoho’s free plan and FreshBooks’ 30-day trial cost nothing, so send a real invoice from each before you decide.

FAQ

Is Zoho Books or FreshBooks cheaper for a freelancer?

Zoho Books, on subscription price. As of Nov 11, 2026, Zoho Books Free costs $0 as long as your revenue for the financial year stays under $50K, and Standard is $15 a month billed annually ($180 a year) or $20 month to month. FreshBooks Lite lists at $23 a month ($276 a year) and was showing 80% off for the first 4 months for new subscribers, about $202.40 in year one. FreshBooks Lite also caps you at 5 billable clients; Zoho caps invoices per year instead (1,000 on Free).

Does Zoho Books have time tracking like FreshBooks?

Not on the cheap plans. Zoho's plan comparison lists projects, timesheets and billing for time from the Professional plan ($40 a month billed annually, $50 monthly). FreshBooks lists unlimited time tracking on every plan, including Lite. If you bill by the hour, that difference matters more than the sticker price.

Which has lower payment fees, Zoho Books or FreshBooks?

For a standard US card they are the same: 2.9% + $0.30 on both FreshBooks Payments and Zoho Payments. Bank transfers are where they split. FreshBooks charges 1% on ACH with no cap (caps only on Select), while Zoho Payments charges 0.8% capped at $5, so a $2,000 invoice paid by ACH costs $20.00 on FreshBooks and $5.00 on Zoho. FreshBooks also charges 3.5% + $0.30 for American Express, commercial and business cards and for AutoPay. Zoho Payments ACH is available on request.

Is the Zoho Books free plan really free?

Yes, with limits. Zoho's pricing page says the Free plan is available indefinitely as long as your revenue for the financial year does not exceed $50K. It covers 1 user plus 1 accountant, up to 1,000 invoices and 1,000 expenses a year, and 50 receipt autoscans a month. Invoices carry Zoho Books branding (removed from Standard), automatic bank feeds start on Standard, and support is email only.

Does either one file my taxes?

No. Both give you the profit and loss report you need for Schedule C, and Zoho Books can prepare 1099s for contractors you pay (e-filing starts on Standard). Neither prepares your income tax return.
