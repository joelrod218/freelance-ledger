The short answer

For a single freelancer with no other income, 2026 federal tax (self-employment tax plus income tax) works out to roughly 15% to 27% of net profit between $20,000 and $200,000 of profit. Add your state's income tax and the common advice to save 25% to 30% of what you earn is a sensible starting point for a lot of people. It is not a law of nature, though: it is too much for many low earners and too little for high earners in high-tax states. The calculator below gives you a number for your own situation.

Self-employment (SE) tax is 15.3% of 92.35% of profit. Income tax uses the 2026 brackets and the $16,100 single / $32,200 joint standard deduction. Read across: a single freelancer with $75,000 of profit and no state income tax would set aside about 21% of profit for federal tax.

Percent of revenue or percent of profit?

Tax is charged on profit (revenue minus business expenses). But if your habit is to move money each time a client pays, the number you actually see is revenue. So the calculator turns your estimate into a share of revenue: if you expect $80,000 of revenue and $10,000 of expenses, the revenue percentage is lower than the profit percentage because part of each payment goes to expenses that are not taxed. Use the revenue percentage for each deposit, then pay your expenses out of the rest.

What the money has to cover

  • Self-employment tax. 15.3% made of 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare, applied to 92.35% of net profit. Social Security stops at the $184,500 wage base for 2026 (shared with any W-2 wages). Medicare has no cap, and an extra 0.9% applies above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint. You owe SE tax once net earnings reach $400 (IRS self-employment tax page). Half of it is deducted when figuring income tax. Our self-employment tax calculator shows this piece on its own.
  • Federal income tax. The 2026 rates are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. For single filers the 12% bracket starts above $12,400, 22% above $50,400 and 24% above $105,700 (joint: $24,800, $100,800, $211,400), per the IRS 2026 inflation adjustments.
  • State and local income tax. Varies a lot, from nothing in states without an income tax to high single digits or more. Enter a rough effective rate; check your state tax agency for its own estimated-payment rules.

The calculator also shows the tax on your next $1,000 of profit. That marginal figure is higher than the average and is the safer rate to use for a big payment late in the year.

When the money leaves

Federal estimated payments for 2026 are due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027 (2026 Form 1040-ES). You can skip the January payment if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the full balance. The IRS also accepts more frequent payments (weekly or monthly) as long as enough is in by each due date. See our estimated taxes guide and the 2027 due dates.

  • Under $1,000: if you expect to owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, estimated payments generally are not required (IRS estimated taxes page). You still owe the tax in April, so keep saving.
  • Safe harbor: you generally avoid the underpayment penalty if withholding plus estimates reach the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 married filing separately). Our quarterly tax calculator works out that minimum.
  • Uneven income: if most of your money arrives late in the year, the annualized income method on Form 2210 can lower the earlier payments.
  • Have a W-2 job too? You can cover freelance tax by asking your employer to withhold more on a new Form W-4 instead of sending estimates.

Where to keep it

The simplest system: a separate savings account that only ever pays the IRS and your state. Move the percentage the day a payment lands, not at the end of the month. If you want buckets for profit, owner's pay and taxes too, try the Profit First calculator, and see business bank accounts for freelancers for accounts with built-in sub-accounts.

Ways to lower the number (legally)

  • Track every deduction. Each $1,000 of business expenses cuts profit by $1,000, which reduces both SE tax and income tax. Start with our freelance tax deductions list, plus the home office and mileage calculators.
  • Retirement contributions. SEP IRA and solo 401(k) contributions can lower income tax (not SE tax). Compare them in solo 401(k) vs SEP IRA.
  • Self-employed health insurance. Premiums you pay may be deductible for income tax. The calculator doesn't include this, so your real bill can be lower.

How the calculator works

  1. Profit = revenue minus expenses.
  2. Self-employment tax on 92.35% of profit (12.4% up to the $184,500 base after any W-2 wages, 2.9% Medicare, 0.9% extra above the threshold).
  3. Adjusted gross income = profit + W-2 wages minus half of SE tax.
  4. Minus the 2026 standard deduction and, if checked, 20% qualified business income deduction (left out above roughly $200,000 single / $400,000 joint of taxable income, where limits apply).
  5. 2026 federal brackets, plus your state percentage applied to adjusted gross income, minus W-2 withholding.
  6. Divided by revenue (per payment) and by profit.

Not included: tax credits, itemized deductions, head of household status, the self-employed health insurance deduction, retirement contributions and the new $400 minimum QBI deduction for 2026. FAQ

Is 25% to 30% the right amount to set aside?

It is a reasonable starting point for many freelancers once state tax is included, but it is a rule of thumb. Federal tax alone for a single filer with no other income runs roughly 15% to 27% of profit between $20,000 and $200,000 in 2026. Lower earners in no-income-tax states can need less; higher earners in high-tax states can need more.

Should I set aside a percentage of revenue or of profit?

If you move money every time a client pays you, use a percentage of revenue, because that is the number hitting your account. Tax is charged on profit, so the revenue percentage is lower when you have real expenses. The calculator shows both.

Does the set-aside cover self-employment tax?

It should. Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of your net profit, and for many freelancers it is a bigger bill than income tax. The calculator includes it.

What if I also have a W-2 job?

Your wages use up part of the $184,500 Social Security wage base for 2026, and the federal tax withheld from your paychecks counts toward what you owe. Enter both and the calculator subtracts the withholding. You can also ask your employer to withhold more on Form W-4 instead of making estimated payments.

Where should I keep the money?

Somewhere separate from your spending money, such as a savings account you only touch to pay the IRS and your state. Many business bank accounts let you create a separate tax bucket.
