Most late payments are paperwork delays, not refusals. Start friendly, keep a dated record of every message, and get firmer only if you hear nothing. The emails below follow that order.

1. Friendly reminder (a few days before the due date)

Subject: Reminder: invoice [#] due [date] Hi [Client name], Just a quick reminder that invoice [#] for [amount] is due on [due date]. You can pay by [payment method / link]. If anything about the invoice needs correcting, or if you need a purchase order number or other detail from me to process it, let me know and I'll turn it around today. Thanks, [Your name]

2. The day after it is due

Subject: Invoice [#] is now due Hi [Client name], Invoice [#] for [amount] was due yesterday, [due date]. I've attached it again and you can pay here: [payment link]. If you've already sent payment, thank you, and please ignore this note. If there's a problem on your end, such as a missing approval or a vendor-setup step, tell me what you need and I'll help get it sorted. Best, [Your name]

3. One to two weeks overdue

Subject: Overdue invoice [#]: [amount] Hi [Client name], I haven't received payment for invoice [#] ([amount]), which was due on [due date] and is now [X] days overdue. A copy is attached. Could you confirm the date I can expect payment? If it's easiest, I'm happy to jump on a five-minute call. Thank you, [Your name]

4. A month overdue: firmer, mentions the agreed late fee

Subject: Second notice: invoice [#] is [X] days overdue Hi [Client name], This is a second notice that invoice [#] for [amount] remains unpaid [X] days after its due date of [due date]. As set out in our agreement, a late fee of [rate] applies to overdue balances, bringing the amount now due to [new total]. I'd like to resolve this by [date, about 5 business days away]. Until payment is received I'll need to pause further work on [project]. Please reply today with a payment date, or tell me if there's an issue I can fix. Regards, [Your name]

5. Final notice before further action

Subject: Final notice: invoice [#] Hi [Client name], I haven't had a response to my earlier messages about invoice [#] for [amount], now [X] days overdue. If I don't receive payment or a written payment plan by [date], I will have to consider further steps to collect, which may include a demand letter or small-claims court. I'd much rather resolve this directly, so please reply by [date]. Regards, [Your name]

Tips that get you paid faster

  • Make paying easy. Put a payment link and the due date at the top of the invoice. Our free invoice generator does the layout.
  • Ask for a deposit on new projects, or split large projects into milestone invoices.
  • Agree late fees in writing before you start (contract or proposal). A fee that appears for the first time on the reminder is harder to collect. Rules on late fees and interest differ by state and country.
  • Find the right person. Often the real obstacle is that your invoice sits with someone in accounts payable who needs a purchase order or vendor form (such as a W-9). Ask.
  • Call. A short call often solves what three emails don't.
  • Know your options. Small-claims courts handle disputes under a state-specific dollar limit. Before threatening anything, check your local rules or ask an attorney.

FAQ

When should I start chasing?

A short reminder a few days before the due date is polite and often enough. Follow up the day after it passes, then weekly.

Can I charge a late fee?

Often yes if it is in your signed agreement and permitted where you operate. Limits on fees and interest vary, so check local rules.

Should I stop work on an unpaid project?

Many freelancers pause work after a set number of days overdue, ideally with this stated in the contract. Say it plainly and give a date.
