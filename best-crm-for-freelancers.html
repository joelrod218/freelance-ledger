“CRM” means two different things to freelancers. Big-company CRMs like HubSpot track contacts and deals. Freelance client tools like HoneyBook, Bonsai, Dubsado and Moxie also run the paperwork that follows a yes: proposal, contract, invoice, deposit and reminders. We compared six options on both, using each company’s own pricing pages on November 17, 2026, and worked out what a year really costs once clients start paying you through them.

Quick picks

  • Cheapest all-in-one: Moxie Starter, $10 a month billed yearly ($120) or $12 month to month. Pipeline, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking and scheduling on the entry plan.
  • Most polished booking flow: HoneyBook Starter, $29 a month billed yearly or $36 monthly. Unlimited clients and projects; scheduler and automations start on Essentials ($49 yearly, $59 monthly).
  • Deepest automations, cheapest ACH: Dubsado Premier, $525 a year. Starter is $335 a year but leaves out automated workflows and scheduling.
  • Projects and time first: Bonsai Essentials, $19 a month billed yearly or $25 monthly. The $9 Basic plan has a CRM but no invoices or contracts.
  • Free contacts and pipeline: HubSpot free CRM (2 users, 1,000 contacts) or Bigin by Zoho Free (1 user, 500 records). Pair either with a free invoicing app.

The six tools, briefly

Moxie

Moxie is built for solo freelancers and packs the most into its entry plan: a sales pipeline, proposals and contracts, invoices, time tracking, schedulers and a shared calendar and inbox for $120 a year. Payments run through your own Stripe or PayPal account rather than a built-in processor, so ACH can be as cheap as Stripe’s 0.8% capped at $5. The trade-off: automations, the white-label client portal and the QuickBooks integration are on Pro ($240 a year).

HoneyBook

HoneyBook is the best fit if your work starts with inquiries: a lead form leads into a proposal that bundles your services, contract and first invoice, which clients can sign and pay in one go. Every plan has unlimited clients and projects. Starter caps you at 2 live lead forms and leaves the scheduler and automations to Essentials, and HoneyBook processes all card and ACH payments itself (other methods can be marked paid manually), with ACH at 1.5% and no cap. Compare it head to head in Bonsai vs HoneyBook and HoneyBook vs FreshBooks.

Dubsado

Dubsado is the power-user pick. Premier adds automated workflows (“automated processes for repetitive tasks,” in Dubsado’s words), unlimited lead forms and scheduling, and Dubsado Payments caps ACH at $5. Expect to spend time building your workflows, and note that the cheaper Starter plan ($335 a year) has no workflows or scheduling, so most people who choose Dubsado for automation end up on Premier ($525).

Bonsai

Bonsai leans toward project work: time tracking, tasks and projects sit at the center, with proposals, contracts and invoices on Essentials. The deals pipeline and custom client fields are Premium features ($29 a month yearly), so if a sales pipeline is the reason you want a CRM, budget for Premium. Bonsai charges 1% on ACH ($1 minimum) and adds a 2.5% FX fee on international payments. See FreshBooks vs Bonsai.

HubSpot free CRM

HubSpot’s free CRM is a real CRM at $0 with no expiration: contacts, deal pipelines, email tracking, meeting links and quotes for up to two users and 1,000 contacts. It is overkill for a few clients and doesn’t handle contracts or invoice payments, but it suits freelancers who run outbound sales or track many prospects at once.

Bigin by Zoho

Bigin is Zoho’s small-business CRM. The free plan is tight (one user, one pipeline, 500 records), but Express at $7 a month billed yearly ($84) adds email integration, 50,000 records and a link to Zoho Invoice, which is free for up to 500 invoices a year. That pairing is the cheapest way to get a proper pipeline plus invoicing if you don’t need contracts in the same tool.

Do you need a CRM at all?

  • Probably not yet if you have a few repeat clients and work comes by referral. A spreadsheet with name, status, next step and date, plus our free invoice generator, will do.
  • Yes if inquiries get lost in your inbox, you rewrite the same proposal and contract every week, or you chase deposits and late invoices by hand. Our unpaid invoice follow-up plan shows the schedule a CRM can automate.
  • Start with the free trials. HoneyBook (30 days), Dubsado (21), Moxie (14), Bigin (15) and Bonsai (7) all let you try before paying; build one real proposal-to-payment flow in each before you commit.

Tax points

  • The subscription and fees are usually deductible. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). Software you use to win and bill clients, and the processing fees on client payments, typically qualify.
  • Report the full invoice, deduct the fee. If a client pays $2,000 by card through HoneyBook and $58.25 is kept as a fee, your income is $2,000 and the fee is an expense. Payments processed through these platforms can also show up on a Form 1099-K; see 1099-NEC vs 1099-K so you don’t count income twice.
  • Export your records. The IRS record-keeping page generally says to keep records for 3 years from when you filed. Download invoices and payout reports at year end, especially before you switch tools.
  • None of these tools files your taxes. Keeper (affiliate link) Standard is $199 a year (bookkeeping plus a federal and up to 2 state returns, reviewed and signed by a tax pro) and Bookkeeping Only is $20 a month, with a 14-day free trial (checked Nov 17, 2026).

Our take

For most solo freelancers, start with Moxie Starter: it is the cheapest way to get pipeline, proposals, contracts and invoices in one place, and ACH through Stripe is capped at $5. Choose HoneyBook if clients find you through inquiries and you want the smoothest sign-and-pay experience, and accept the higher ACH fee. Choose Dubsado Premier if you will invest a weekend in automations. If you only need to remember who to follow up with, use HubSpot’s free CRM or Bigin Free and keep invoicing in a separate app.

FAQ

What is the best CRM for a solo freelancer?

It depends on what you want the CRM to do. If you want one tool that takes an inquiry through proposal, contract, invoice and payment, Moxie is the cheapest we found ($10 a month billed yearly as of Nov 17, 2026), HoneyBook has the most polished booking flow ($29 a month billed yearly), and Dubsado has the deepest automations on its $525-a-year Premier plan. If you only need contacts, notes and a deal pipeline, HubSpot's free CRM or Bigin's free plan cost nothing.

Is there a free CRM for freelancers?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM has no expiration date and covers up to two users and 1,000 contacts, with deal pipelines, email tracking, meeting scheduling and quotes. Bigin by Zoho has a free plan for a single user with one pipeline and 500 records. Neither is built to send contracts or collect invoice payments for you, so most freelancers pair them with a separate invoicing app.

Do I need a CRM as a freelancer?

Not always. If you have a handful of repeat clients, a spreadsheet and a calendar are enough. A CRM starts paying off when inquiries slip through the cracks, when you send the same proposal and contract over and over, or when you chase deposits by hand. That is the point where an all-in-one client tool saves real hours.

Which freelance CRM has the lowest payment fees?

On bank transfers (ACH), Dubsado Payments and Moxie (through your own Stripe account) charge 0.8% capped at $5, so a $2,000 ACH payment costs $5. Bonsai charges 1% ($20) and HoneyBook 1.5% ($30), as of Nov 17, 2026. Card fees are close everywhere, about 2.9% plus 25 to 30 cents.

Is CRM software tax deductible?

Usually, if you use it for your business. IRS Publication 334 says a business expense must be ordinary (common and accepted in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate). A CRM subscription and its payment processing fees for client work typically qualify. Keep the receipts; the IRS generally says to keep records for 3 years from when you filed.
